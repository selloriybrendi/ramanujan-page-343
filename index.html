θ = 21/5, a = 1/(θ−1) = 1+θ+θ²+θ³+θ⁴, b = √5/(1+θ²)5/2 = 1−θ−θ²+θ⁴. Ramanujan claimed that ambnθ is extremely close to an integer pm,n. Andrews–Berndt (Lost Notebook IV, §7.4) called the claims wrong; the integer is the trace and the error is a sum of Galois conjugates. self-test …

1. Calculator for (7.4.1)

pm,n = Tr(ambnθ) is computed exactly with integer arithmetic in Z[θ]. ε = ambnθ − pm,n = −Σs=1..4 σs(ambnθ) is computed from the conjugates, so no big-float library is needed. Theorem: |ε| ≤ 2θ(|σ₁(a)|m|σ₁(b)|n + |σ₂(a)|m|σ₂(b)|n); the error tends to 0 exactly when m > 6.0114·n.

2. Why 5 and 7: the Pisot boundary

ap = 1/(21/p−1) is a unit. It is a Pisot number (all other conjugates inside the unit circle) exactly when 21/p > 2cos(2π/p), i.e. p ≤ 6. Ramanujan's two cases are p = 5 (last odd Pisot case) and p = 7 (first non-Pisot case, where he adds the units b and c).
